SIH26231 · Narcotics Control Bureau · Ministry of Home Affairs

Digital Companion for Field Drug Testing

You are not building a chemistry lab. You are building a phone witness.

One sentence. Police already use a tiny kit that changes colour. Your app photographs that colour fairly, names the result, and locks the photo to a time, place, and officer so nobody can quietly swap it later.

What exists today

Officers carry a field kit. A drop of unknown stuff goes in. The kit changes colour. Humans squint at it under streetlights and argue whether it is “purple enough.” A notebook line can be written later. Courts do not love “trust me, I saw purple.”

The job, as four steps

StepOfficerYour app
1. Kit Uses the official colour-change kit Does no chemistry. The kit already exists.
2. Photo Puts kit + printed colour card in one picture Uses the card to correct weird lighting
3. Call Waits a second instead of squinting Says Positive, Negative, or Inconclusive
4. Lock Gets a record they can show later Saves GPS, time, officer ID, photo, and a hash (fingerprint) of the photo

The colour card

Same trick photographers use. Known colours in the shot tell the computer how yellow or dim the room is. Then it compares the vial to those known colours instead of trusting the phone’s lying pixels.

The sealed envelope

A hash is a fingerprint of the exact photo file. Change one pixel, fingerprint changes. GPS and time answer where and when. Together that is closer to evidence than a WhatsApp screenshot.

Do not claim this convicts anyone

NCB says the result is presumptive — a first look, like a smoke alarm. A real lab still confirms. Write that on the screen. Honesty is part of the brief.

Hackathon demo (no real drugs)

Use a pH strip, food colouring in a vial, or printed colour circles next to a paper colour card. Label it “simulated kit.” The puzzle is the same: a coloured blob + a reference card + messy light.

Sentence to remember while building.
You are not detecting drugs. You are detecting a colour fairly, then refusing to let that photo become a rumour.
