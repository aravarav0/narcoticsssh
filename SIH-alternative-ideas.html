Backups only · still software · still finishable in a 12-hour sprint

Other problems with similar potential

First choice is still SIH26231 (field drug-test companion). These are the next-best software briefs if your team hates cameras, wants something more playable, or wants an even drier government tool that fewer campus teams will copy.

What “similar potential” means here: you can demo a real product overnight, the official brief is a short checklist (not “build a national satellite GIS”), and it is not a Student Innovation free-for-all. Hardware is still out.

1. Closest cousin — also “photo + official record”

SIH26034 — Label cop: is this packet legal?

Ministry of Consumer Affairs · Software · scan a packet, check the law

Every biscuit and shampoo bottle in India must print certain things: MRP, net weight, manufacturer, month of manufacture, veg/non-veg mark, and so on. Inspectors currently squint at labels. You build an app: photograph the pack, pull the text (OCR), tick which mandatory fields are missing, spit out a compliance report.

  • Why it can win: Same shape as 26231 — camera, rules, PDF report, officer dashboard. Unsexy, so fewer copycats.
  • 12-hour demo: Upload 3 real packets from your hostel. Show “MRP missing” / “all good” / PDF.
  • Pick this if: The team likes forms, OCR, and checklists more than colour science.
  • Risk: The legal rulebook is long. Do not try to encode the entire Act. Encode 6–8 must-have fields and say so.

2. Highest wow — judges can play it

SIH26003 — Memory games for elderly people in the North-East

MDoNER · Software · MedTech · dementia / caregiver app

Older people with memory loss need simple games (match cards, remember a daily routine) plus medicine reminders. A caregiver sees a dashboard: “Dad played 12 minutes, missed the 8pm pill.” Add big buttons, a regional language toggle, and a fake “offline” mode.

  • Why it can win: Live play in front of judges. Emotional story. Overnight you can ship 2 games + reminders + one dashboard.
  • 12-hour demo: One “patient” phone, one “daughter” dashboard, one voice reminder.
  • Pick this if: You have designers / frontend people and almost nobody wants computer vision.
  • Risk: Easy to look like a generic memory-match toy. Lean hard on NER languages, huge type, and the caregiver side — that is the actual problem.

3. Easiest to actually finish

SIH26032 — Farmers book a slot instead of standing in a 6-hour queue

Department of Consumer Affairs · Software · procurement centre queue

Harvest time: hundreds of farmers at a government buy centre, no idea when their turn is, no idea if payment went through. You build: register, pick a time slot, live queue number, SMS/app “come in 20 minutes,” payment status.

  • Why it can win: The official expected solution is already your MVP list. Six people can ship this.
  • 12-hour demo: Farmer app + centre desk screen showing the queue. Fake 40 farmers. SMS can be a mock inbox if APIs are slow.
  • Pick this if: The team is mixed-skill / fresher-heavy and you want something that will exist at 10am.
  • Risk: Looks like a college database assignment unless the queue UI is excellent and you show the farmer’s anxiety clearly.

4. Same “help a real worker with a phone” energy

SIH26090 — Artisan takes a messy photo, app writes the Amazon listing

MoSJE · Software · Heritage · weavers / craftspeople who are not “digital”

A weaver can make a shawl and cannot write an English product page. App: snap photo, clean background, artisan speaks a description in their language, you turn it into a title + price suggestion + catalogue. Think “Canva + translate + shop” for low literacy.

  • Why it can win: Beautiful demo. Camera + voice + listing page. Heritage theme is less crowded than “AI disaster.”
  • 12-hour demo: One craft photo → pretty card → Hindi/English listing. Voice can be one language you actually speak.
  • Pick this if: You have a designer and someone who can call an image/LLM API without melting.
  • Risk: If the APIs fail at 3am you have nothing. Have an offline fallback (templates + manual text).

SIH26229 — Kabadiwala Connect: scrap collector meets authorised recycler

Ministry of Mines · Software · e-waste · fair price + handover receipt

Informal collectors (kabadiwalas) do not know a fair price or which recycler is legal. Phone app, big buttons, photo of a pile of cables/PCBs, estimated value, nearest authorised recycler, a paper-like receipt when they hand it over. Offline-ish for cheap phones.

  • Why it can win: Clear villain (unsafe backyard recycling) and a simple marketplace. Strong “India problem” story.
  • 12-hour demo: Collector flow (photo → price) + recycler flow (accept lot → receipt). Fake price table is fine.
  • Pick this if: You like maps, simple marketplaces, and a green-impact pitch.
  • Risk: The full brief is huge (EPR, lots of material types). Ship one material (e.g. cables + batteries) extremely well.

SIH26089 — Cooperative Urban Company: plumber/electrician booking, but owned by a co-op

Ministry of Cooperation · Software · gig work without being Uber-for-exploiting-workers

Urban Company clone on the surface: book an electrician. Difference you must shout: workers belong to a labour cooperative, verification, fair wage, welfare toggle — not a private gig app extracting commission.

  • Why it can win: Everyone understands “book a plumber.” Overnight: list, book, track, rate, co-op admin.
  • Pick this if: You have no ML person and still want a product judges already know how to click.
  • Risk: If you forget the cooperative angle it is just another clone and you lose on originality.

5. Solid, a bit more “website than invention”

SIH26075 — CAPACITY CONNECT: training portal for IMD / Earth Sciences staff

India Meteorological Department · Software · LMS · trainee / trainer / admin

Login as three roles. Trainee takes courses and MCQs. Trainer uploads slides and quizzes. Admin sees dashboards and announces things. It is Moodle, but for a ministry, which is what they asked for.

  • Why it can win: Spec is unusually concrete. A complete LMS beats a fake AI satellite.
  • Pick this if: The team is strongest at full-stack web and weakest at “wow.”
  • Risk: Generic. You must skin it as IMD (weather, field staff, certificates) or it looks like every tutorial.

Use these only if you already have the skill

SIH26041 — Phone AR safety training for Jharkhand mines

Highest ceiling, lowest floor. They want a working Android APK with AR overlays (fire exit, gas leak). If AR works on a mid-range phone, you look like wizards. If tracking jitters, you have a broken camera app. Do not pick this to “try AR this weekend.”

Still skip: all Hardware PSs, Student Innovation (tourism / toys / fitness catch-alls), landslide GIS (SIH26001), and fake-passport AI (SIH26188). Those look impressive on paper and usually die as unfinished decks.
