Teammate brief: Digital Companion for Field Drug Testing

We are building a phone app that sits next to an existing colour-change kit. We do not invent chemistry. We photograph the result fairly, name it (positive / negative / inconclusive), and lock the photo so it cannot be quietly swapped. Official line: this is presumptive. A lab still confirms. Demo with pH strips / food colouring / printed swatches labelled “simulated kit.”

Background:
Field drug-testing kits currently in use rely on visual interpretation of a
colour-change reaction. This makes results subjective, difficult to
standardise across officers, and leaves no verifiable record that a test was
actually conducted at a given place and time. As a result, field test
outcomes cannot presently be relied upon as documentary evidence.

Description:
Participants are to build a mobile application that works alongside existing
colorimetric field-test kits (no new hardware).

The application should:
• Capture an image of the test result using the device camera, using a
  reference colour card in-frame for lighting calibration.
• Automatically classify the result against a defined set of outcome
  categories (e.g., positive, negative, inconclusive).
• Generate a tamper-evident digital record of the test, capturing timestamp,
  GPS location, and an operator identifier, together with a cryptographic
  hash of the captured image.
• Maintain a simple, searchable log of tests conducted.

Expected deliverables:
A working mobile/web application prototype demonstrating image capture,
automated result classification, and generation of a signed digital record.

Note:
The output of this application is a presumptive field-test result and a
supporting digital record; it does not replace laboratory confirmatory testing.

2. What NCB is actually angry about

  1. Eyes disagree. Streetlight vs fluorescent vs dusk. Two officers, same vial, different call.
  2. No sealed photo. A notebook line can be written later. Courts do not treat “I saw purple” as a document.

Our app is a witness: fair colour + wax-seal on the file. It is not a conviction machine.

3. Existing solutions (what papers already did)

Nobody published “the SIH26231 winner.” Colour-from-phone is a known lab field. Steal the methods, not the chemistry.

ApproachWhat they didSteal for us
Color card → 3×3 matrix Photograph a Macbeth/Spyder-style card; least-squares map camera RGB → CIE XYZ / Lab. Burggraaff et al. 2020; 2025 kinetic RGB correction papers. This is exactly what NCB asked: card in the same frame.
Device-specific calibration Same phone model ≠ same mapping. Two Nexuses already disagree. IS&T 2019 Do not ship one matrix for “all Androids.” Calibrate per capture using the in-frame card.
Hue + saturation thresholds Forensic microfluidic + phone: H and S used as pass/fail for presumptive colour tests (Anal. Chem. 2016). Overnight MVP: after correction, classify on H and S, not raw RGB.
Saturation-only (HSV S) Coleman et al. — S beat other phone colorimetry tricks across 10k images in messy light. When the kit goes from “almost colourless” to “strongly coloured,” S is a strong feature.
CIELAB a*, b* Biosensor paper: a*/b* more lighting-stable than RGB (“equichromatic surfaces”). After card correction, compare a*, b* to printed positive/negative swatches.
Flash minus ambient Two shots: flash on / off; subtract. SSNR < 3.4 → retake. Burggraaff 2020. Nice stretch. For SSH, one shot + card is enough if we refuse bad photos.
Time series / ΔE over seconds RSC 2023 “old presumptive tests, new digital tricks” — hue and ΔE evolve; mixtures look different from pure. Research topic, not 12-hour MVP. Video of colour developing is a wow if we have time.
pH-strip style 2D xy calibration Shen / Kim: reference patches + CIE xy plane so auto-WB does not wreck the reading. Same idea as our colour card.

4. Ratios and numbers we can actually use

Never classify on raw JPEG R,G,B. Linearise sRGB first (undo gamma ~2.2), then pick features that ignore “how bright the room is.”

NameFormula (idea)Why
Chromaticity r, g r = R/(R+G+B), g = G/(R+G+B) Divides out overall brightness. Classic cheap illumination invariance.
Channel ratios R/G, R/B, G/B Simple. Unstable if one channel clips at 0 or 255.
White-balance (von Kries) Divide R,G,B by the gray-patch R,G,B from the card Minimum viable “card calibration.” NCB’s lighting sentence in one line.
3×3 colour correction matrix XYZ ≈ M · RGB, M from least squares on card patches Best standard method. Needs ≥3–6 known patches (white, black, RGB, expected kit colours).
Hue H (HSV) Angle on the colour wheel, 0–360° Answers “is it purple-ish vs orange-ish?” Mostly independent of dim vs bright.
Saturation S How far from gray “Did anything actually happen?” Negative kits stay dull; positives get punchy.
CIE Lab L*, a*, b* L* = light/dark; a* = green↔red; b* = blue↔yellow Human-ish distances. Classify on a*, b*; treat wild L* as “lighting problem.”
ΔE (CIE76 or CIEDE2000) Distance from sample Lab to each reference swatch Nearest swatch wins. If two nearest are too close → inconclusive.
Absorbance analogue A = −log10(I_sample / I_blank) per channel, blank = negative well or white patch More “chemistry-paper.” Useful if we ever quantify strength, not just pos/neg.
Overnight recipe (enough to win the demo):
1) Detect card patches + vial ROI (overlay guides in the camera UI).
2) Average ≥30 pixels (one pixel is noise).
3) Linearise. Divide by gray patch (von Kries).
4) Convert to HSV + Lab.
5) ΔE to three printed swatches: negative / positive / muddy.
6) If min ΔE is large, or top-two ΔEs are close, or card missing, or highlight clipped → inconclusive.

6. Research problems to split among teammates

Pick one each. Short literature note + how it changes our app. Do not all pile on “use YOLO.”

  1. Which feature is most lighting-stable on our simulated kit? Chromaticity vs H vs S vs a*b* vs ΔE. Shoot the same vial under yellow lamp / daylight / flash, with and without card. Table of error rates. (This is the money experiment for the PPT.)
  2. When must we say inconclusive? Thresholds: missing card, SSNR, ΔE margin, clip %, card pixel count. Honesty is in the official note.
  3. Colour correction matrix vs gray-patch-only. How many patches do we need? 1 gray vs 6 vs 24.
  4. Print quality. Laser vs inkjet vs phone-screen “card.” How wrong does a bad print make ΔE?
  5. ROI. Manual box vs contour vs “must fill this circle.” Average vs median pixels (median survives glare better).
  6. Tamper-evident record, legally. SHA-256 of the JPEG is a fingerprint, not a court-admissible signature. Research: hash the raw bytes, store GPS+time+officer, maybe sign with a device key. What GPS spoofing does. What “documentary evidence” might still fail. Stay humble on the UI: “presumptive record,” not “proof.”
  7. Why field colour tests are controversial. High-level only: visual kits have known false positives; that is why NCB says lab confirmation. We do not research how to fake a kit. We research why an app must never claim certainty.
  8. Stretch: two-frame flash subtraction; or 5-second video of colour developing (ΔE over time) from the RSC 2023 paper.
