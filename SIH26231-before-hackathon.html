0. People (5 minutes)

1. The “red paper” — it is a COLOUR CARD, not one red sheet

One red paper is useless. The phone needs known colours in the same photo as the test so it can tell “this room is yellow-ish.” You print one A4 page with 6 fat squares.

SquareWhat to printWhy
WhitePlain white paper colour. RGB 255,255,255Brightest reference
BlackRGB 0,0,0 or as dark as the printer goesDarkest reference
GrayRGB 128,128,128 (middle gray)The important one. Used to fix lighting
RedRGB 224, 32, 32. Strong red, not pink, not maroonTells the phone what “red” is in this light
YellowRGB 240, 208, 32Streetlights are yellow. We need this
PurpleRGB 112, 48, 160. Not blue, not pinkMany kit “positives” look purplish. Fake target colour
How to make it (stupid version)
  1. Open Google Slides or PowerPoint. Page = A4 landscape or portrait, does not matter.
  2. Draw 6 squares. Each square at least 4 cm × 4 cm (bigger is better, like a biscuit). No tiny stamps.
  3. Fill with the 6 colours above. Put the name under each square in huge letters: WHITE BLACK GRAY RED YELLOW PURPLE.
  4. Leave a white margin around the page so you can hold it without covering squares.
  5. Print in colour. Ask for matte / normal paper, not glossy photo paper (gloss = camera glare = app cries).
  6. Print 3 copies. One will get wet. One stays in the bag as backup.
  7. Do not laminate. Do not put it behind shiny plastic.
  8. If the campus printer is trash, buy: 1 white cardstock, 1 pack of coloured paper (red, yellow, purple, black) and tape 4 cm squares onto white A4. Ugly is fine. Same sizes.
